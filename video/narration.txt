[SCENE 1]
Regulatory reporting is not only about the final field. Accountability depends on reconstructing which source supported the number, and what happened when the record did not agree.

[SCENE 2]
KRISEVA ATTEST is a research-stage prototype: one fictional fund manager, three synthetic sources, three fields. It files nothing and has no IFSCA connection.

[SCENE 3]
For closing NAV, two normalized values agree. The prototype labels the field supported while keeping the source references and fingerprints beside the candidate evidence.

[SCENE 4]
Committed capital is different. The administrator statement shows fifty million rupees; the board schedule shows forty-eight million. ATTEST preserves both, marks a conflict, and proposes no winner. When investor-complaint evidence is absent, the deterministic trace abstains instead of inventing a value.

[SCENE 5]
A named reviewer must choose an action, identify the source when required, and record a reason. The decision is explicit and attributed. This demonstration does not interpret regulation, determine compliance, or replace the accountable human.

[SCENE 6]
The receipt retains fingerprints, both candidates, the human decision, and the unresolved request. Next is practitioner testing with fund managers and administrators before any decision to lock a company wedge.
